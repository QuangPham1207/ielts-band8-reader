W-T2-02 — Discussion: Who Should Pay for Healthcare?

Writing Task 2 · Discussion + opinion · 289 words · 40 minutes · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. Some believe healthcare should be funded entirely by the government, while others think individuals should pay for their own care. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

Some argue that medical care should be paid for entirely from public funds, while others insist that patients should cover their own treatment. Both positions respond to real pressures, but I believe a tax-funded system with modest individual contributions is the most workable arrangement. This essay will consider the arguments for each approach before explaining my own view.

Supporters of full public funding argue that health protection should not depend on income. When treatment is free at the point of use, poorer patients seek help earlier, which makes conditions cheaper to treat and reduces pressure on emergency services. Collective funding also spreads the risk of expensive illness: a cancer patient could never afford months of specialist care alone, but a national insurance pool absorbs such shocks. Countries with universal systems, such as Japan, achieve long life expectancy without leaving patients with crushing bills. The underlying principle is that a healthy workforce benefits the whole economy, so the cost is fairly shared through taxation.

On the other hand, critics warn that entirely free care encourages overuse and strains budgets. If every consultation and prescription costs the patient nothing, some people visit doctors for minor complaints that rest or a pharmacist could resolve, lengthening waiting times for urgent cases. Charging fees, they argue, makes patients weigh necessity and choose responsibly, while generating revenue that can fund hospitals. A partial fee for non-essential visits, for instance, has helped several health systems control demand without denying emergency treatment. This discipline, supporters claim, keeps the service financially sustainable.

In my view, neither extreme works in isolation. A universal guarantee should cover essential and emergency care, while small charges for routine or elective services can discourage waste. This balance preserves fairness while protecting public budgets.

2. Why it scores

  • TR: Band 8 — both views are developed symmetrically, then a distinct personal position is stated and repeated ("neither extreme works in isolation", "This balance preserves fairness"). Addresses all parts with extended, supported ideas.
  • CC: Band 8 — each body paragraph opens with its stance and chains cause to effect ("which makes conditions cheaper", "lengthening waiting times"). Contrast is handled by paragraph logic ("On the other hand") rather than a linker chain; mechanical over-linking would cap CC at 6–7.
  • LR: Band 8 — flexible health-system range used precisely: "free at the point of use", "spreads the risk", "crushing bills", "financially sustainable". A memorised phrase dropped regardless of context would cap LR as a collocation error.
  • GRA: Band 8 — wide range including conditionals ("If every consultation… costs the patient nothing"), concessive clauses ("without denying emergency treatment") and cleft-like emphasis. Majority error-free; occasional complexity is controlled.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: When + access condition, + group behaviour + system result — example: "When treatment is free at the point of use, poorer patients seek help earlier, which makes conditions cheaper to treat". Job: links a policy design to human behaviour to fiscal outcome in one chain.
  • Frame: If + zero-price condition, + overuse behaviour + consequence — example: "If every consultation and prescription costs the patient nothing, some people visit doctors for minor complaints…, lengthening waiting times". Job: builds the critics' case as a testable mechanism, not a slogan.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Intro: paraphrase both views → own balanced position → map (view A, view B, verdict)
  2. Body 1: view A (equity + early treatment) → risk-pooling mechanism → country example → principle
  3. Body 2: view B (overuse + budgets) → behavioural mechanism → partial-fee example → sustainability claim
  4. Conclusion: reject both extremes → blended solution (guarantee + small charges)

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Retell the body-2 mechanism in 3 sentences for a new topic (e.g. free university tuition causing over-enrolment): zero price → behaviour change → system consequence. Keep the If-frame, change all content words. 10 minutes.
Do not copy: "Countries with universal systems, such as Japan, achieve long life expectancy without leaving patients with crushing bills" — works only here because the prompt is about health funding; transplanting Japan into another topic would cap TR for irrelevance.
