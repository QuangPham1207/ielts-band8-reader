W-T2-01 — Opinion: Are Home Workers More Productive?

Writing Task 2 · Opinion (agree / disagree) · 283 words · 40 minutes · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. Employees who work from home are more productive than those who work in offices. To what extent do you agree or disagree? Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

Many managers now claim that staff who work remotely achieve more than colleagues based in a central office. I largely agree with this view, although the advantage depends on the nature of the job and the discipline of the individual. This essay will explain the gains in focus before addressing the limits of remote collaboration.

The main reason is that home working protects long periods of concentrated effort. In an open-plan office, employees are interrupted by meetings, conversations and impromptu requests, which fragments attention and forces them to restart tasks repeatedly. At home, by contrast, they can organise the day around demanding work, answering messages in batches rather than reacting instantly. A software analyst, for example, may complete a complex report in one quiet morning at home that would take two days amid office distractions. The result is not merely faster output but fewer errors caused by constant task-switching.

Admittedly, remote work suits self-directed tasks better than collaborative ones. Junior employees who need supervision, or teams designing a product together, often progress faster face to face, where misunderstandings can be resolved immediately and motivation is visible. However, this limitation can be managed through regular video check-ins, shared deadlines and occasional office days for joint work. When such routines are in place, the productivity lost to isolation is smaller than the time regained from commuting and unnecessary meetings. For this reason, the office remains useful for coordination, yet it need not be the default workplace.

Taken together, home working tends to raise output because it preserves focus and removes commuting, provided employers maintain clear expectations and contact. Offices still matter for teamwork, but for most individual tasks the home is the more productive setting.

2. Why it scores

  • TR: Band 8 — takes a clear qualified position and sustains it throughout ("I largely agree", "the office remains useful for coordination, yet it need not be the default workplace"). Both sides of the claim are weighed, so the response "sufficiently addresses all parts" with extended, supported ideas.
  • CC: Band 8 — ideas sequence logically within paragraphs; concession is signalled without mechanical chains ("Admittedly", "At home, by contrast", "For this reason"). Each paragraph has one central topic. A copied linker chain here would cap CC at 6–7 for mechanical cohesion.
  • LR: Band 8 — fluent, flexible range with skilful uncommon items used naturally: "fragments attention", "impromptu requests", "constant task-switching". A memorised synonym swapped out of context (e.g. "productive" for every gain) would cap LR through collocation error.
  • GRA: Band 8 — wide range: relative clauses ("which fragments attention"), concessive clauses ("provided employers maintain"), conditionals ("When such routines are in place"). Majority of sentences error-free. Memorised complex frames forced onto new content typically break here and cap GRA.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: When + routine condition, + comparative result — example: "When such routines are in place, the productivity lost to isolation is smaller than the time regained from commuting". Job: concedes a drawback, then outweighs it with a measured comparison.
  • Frame: X remains useful for Y, yet need not be Z — example: "the office remains useful for coordination, yet it need not be the default workplace". Job: qualifies agreement elegantly; keeps a clear position while limiting its scope.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Intro: paraphrase claim → qualified agree + scope limit → essay map (gains, then limits)
  2. Body 1: main gain (focus) → mechanism (fewer interruptions) → concrete example → result (speed + accuracy)
  3. Body 2: concession (collaboration weaker) → management fix (routines + office days) → weighing (loss smaller than gain)
  4. Conclusion: restate qualified position → condition for success

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Rewrite body paragraph 1 for a different claim (e.g. four-day weeks raise output) reusing only the move: mechanism → concrete worker example → dual result (speed + quality). Time yourself: 10 minutes.
Do not copy: "A software analyst, for example, may complete a complex report in one quiet morning at home" — works only here because the prompt is about home vs office focus; copying the analyst into another topic would cap TR for unanswered parts.
