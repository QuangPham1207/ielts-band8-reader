W-T1-01 — LINE GRAPH: ELECTRICITY CONSUMPTION BY SECTOR

Writing Task 1 · Line graph · ~167 words · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. The line graph below shows annual electricity consumption, in terawatt-hours (TWh), by the residential, industrial and commercial sectors in the fictional country of Aldavia between 2000 and 2020. The three series start at approximately 240 TWh (residential), 480 TWh (industrial) and 150 TWh (commercial); industry peaks near 560 TWh around 2010 then levels off, residential stays broadly flat, and commercial rises steadily to about 380 TWh. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words. Time target: 20 minutes.

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

The line graph compares annual electricity consumption, measured in terawatt-hours (TWh), across residential, industrial and commercial users between 2000 and 2020.

Overall, industrial demand remained the largest source of consumption throughout the period, while commercial use recorded the fastest growth. Residential consumption changed comparatively little, so the gap between the three sectors narrowed markedly over time.

In 2000, industry consumed around 480 TWh, roughly double the residential figure of 240 TWh. Industrial demand then rose steadily to a peak of approximately 560 TWh in 2010 before levelling off, ending the period at about 540 TWh. Residential use followed a flatter path, edging up to 280 TWh by 2008 and remaining broadly stable thereafter.

Commercial consumption, initially the smallest at 150 TWh, increased in every successive five-year interval. It overtook residential use in around 2014 and reached 380 TWh by 2020, almost closing the gap with industrial demand. As a result, commercial demand more than doubled across the twenty years, whereas residential demand grew by less than one-fifth.

2. Why it scores

  • TA: Band 8 — key features clearly presented and highlighted. The overview gives two number-free trends ("remained the largest source" + "recorded the fastest growth"), and every detail paragraph selects peaks, crossovers and endpoints instead of listing each year. A memorised overview with figures pasted in would cap TA at 6–7 for failing to highlight clearly.
  • CC: Band 8 — information sequenced logically: paraphrase, overview, then one paragraph per grouping (flat sectors vs. riser). "...before levelling off, ending the period at..." chains stages inside one sentence rather than stacking linkers. Copying a "Moreover / Furthermore" chain here would cap CC at 6 for mechanical cohesion.
  • LR: Band 8 — flexible, precise range: "...remained the largest source of consumption...", "...narrowed markedly...", "...followed a flatter path...". Collocations fit energy data exactly. A pre-learned phrase such as "skyrocketed dramatically" applied to the flat residential line would cap LR at 6–7 as a collocation error.
  • GRA: Band 8 — wide range with majority error-free sentences: participle clause ("...ending the period at about 540 TWh"), contrast clause ("...whereas residential demand grew by less than one-fifth"), and prepositional precision throughout. No systematic error pattern.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: X rose to a peak of Y in [year] before + -ing — example: "rose steadily to a peak of approximately 560 TWh in 2010 before levelling off". Job: compresses three facts (rise, peak value, subsequent plateau) into one sentence.
  • Frame: Whereas + clause, ideal for final contrast — example: "whereas residential demand grew by less than one-fifth". Job: delivers the comparison the task explicitly rewards, without starting a new paragraph.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Paraphrase: what is measured, units, sectors, dates.
  2. Overview: rank statement (who stays top) + change statement (who moves fastest / who is flat), no numbers.
  3. Detail A: the two flatter series grouped — start values, peak/plateau, end values.
  4. Detail B: the fast riser alone — start, crossover point, end, proportional summary.

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Cover the graph and rewrite the overview from memory in one sentence contrasting rank and speed, then check you used zero figures.
Do not copy: "It overtook residential use in around 2014" — works only here because only this dataset has that crossover year and those two sectors.
