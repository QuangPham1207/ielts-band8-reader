W-T1-02 — BAR CHART: RECYCLING RATES BY MATERIAL AND COUNTRY

Writing Task 1 · Bar chart · ~175 words · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. The bar chart below compares the proportion of waste recycled, in percent, for four materials (paper, glass, plastic and metal) in four fictional countries (Alveria, Brenna, Caldora and Dovria) in 2022. Paper leads everywhere (55–78%); plastic trails everywhere (24–39%); Brenna is top in three materials and Caldora bottom overall. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words. Time target: 20 minutes.

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

The bar chart compares recycling rates for four materials — paper, glass, plastic and metal — in four countries, Alveria, Brenna, Caldora and Dovria, in 2022. Figures are given as percentages of each material's waste recycled.

Overall, paper was the most widely recycled material in every country, while plastic recorded the lowest rate in all four. Brenna achieved the highest rates for three of the four materials, whereas Caldora generally recycled the least.

For paper, Brenna led at 78%, followed by Dovria at 71%, Alveria at 64% and Caldora at 55%. A similar ranking appeared for glass, with Brenna recycling 69% against 42% in Caldora, while Alveria and Dovria stood at 58% and 61% respectively.

Metal recycling ranged from 48% in Caldora to 66% in Brenna. Plastic showed both the lowest and narrowest range of figures: only 24% in Caldora and 31% in Alveria, rising to 35% in Dovria and 39% in Brenna. Consequently, even the best performer recycled less than two-fifths of its plastic, in sharp contrast to the much higher recovery of paper.

2. Why it scores

  • TA: Band 8 — all requirements covered with clear highlighting: material ranking (paper top, plastic bottom) plus country ranking (Brenna top, Caldora bottom). Details select extremes and ranges rather than reciting all 16 bars. Listing every bar mechanically would cap TA at 6–7 for burying key features.
  • CC: Band 8 — paragraphs grouped by idea: overview (two rankings), paper/glass together, metal/plastic together. "A similar ranking appeared for glass" links paragraphs without a linker chain. Inserting "Moreover / Furthermore" between every sentence would cap CC at 6 for mechanical cohesion.
  • LR: Band 8 — skilful uncommon items used naturally: "...the most widely recycled material...", "...the narrowest range of figures...", "...in sharp contrast to...". Each fits bar-chart comparison exactly. A memorised "double-edged sword" style phrase would cap LR at 6–7 as irrelevant lexis.
  • GRA: Band 8 — range includes ranking clause ("...followed by Dovria at 71%..."), "with + -ing" adjunct ("...with Brenna recycling 69% against 42%..."), and concessive end-weight ("...even the best performer recycled less than..."). Majority error-free.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: with X verb-ing Y against Z — example: "with Brenna recycling 69% against 42% in Caldora". Job: packs best-vs-worst comparison into a subordinate phrase, saving a whole sentence.
  • Frame: even the best performer + past-simple understatement — example: "even the best performer recycled less than two-fifths of its plastic". Job: turns a maximum figure into an evaluative conclusion, which is what TA rewards.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Paraphrase: materials, countries, year, unit (percent recycled).
  2. Overview: material ranking + country ranking, no numbers.
  3. Detail A: top materials grouped (paper, then glass as "similar ranking") with full ranking.
  4. Detail B: lower materials grouped (range for metal, lowest + narrow range for plastic) + concluding contrast.

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Rewrite detail paragraph 2 using only ranges (highest–lowest) instead of all four country figures, keeping the same grouping logic.
Do not copy: "only 24% in Caldora and 31% in Alveria" — works only here because these exact plastic figures belong to this invented dataset.
