W-T1-05 — MAPS: TOWN CENTRE IN 2000 AND TODAY

Writing Task 1 · Maps (before / after) · ~183 words · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. The two maps below show the fictional town centre of Millford in 2000 and today. The open-air market on the central square has been replaced by a covered mall; shops on High Street (north) are now restaurants; a ring road circles the centre and Station Road is pedestrianised; the town park (south) is halved for a car park; the railway station (south-east) is unchanged; the post office (east) is gone from the description. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words. Time target: 20 minutes.

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

The maps compare the centre of the town of Millford in 2000 with its layout today.

Overall, the town centre has been extensively redeveloped for retail and traffic flow, with green space reduced and several older commercial buildings replaced. The railway station is the principal feature to have remained in place, while road access around the centre has been completely reorganised.

In 2000, an open-air market occupied the central square, flanked by small shops along High Street to the north and a post office to the east. A modest town park lay to the south of the market, and through-traffic passed directly along Station Road in front of the station, which stood in the south-east.

Today, the market has been demolished and replaced by a covered shopping mall on the same site, while the shops on High Street have been converted into restaurants. A ring road now circles the centre, diverting traffic away from Station Road, which is pedestrianised. The park has been reduced to roughly half its former size to make way for a car park, although the station itself is unchanged.

2. Why it scores

  • TA: Band 8 — all major changes selected: market-to-mall, shops-to-restaurants, ring road + pedestrianisation, halved park, unchanged station. Overview characterises the change (retail + traffic) and names the exception. Describing only one map would cap TA at 5–6 for ignoring comparison.
  • CC: Band 8 — spatial logic carries cohesion: overview (theme + exception), then 2000 layout, then matched today layout in the same order (centre, north, south, roads). "...on the same site..." and "...itself..." track places without repeated full nouns. Listing changes in random order with "Moreover" links would cap CC at 6.
  • LR: Band 8 — location and change lexis used precisely: "...flanked by small shops along High Street to the north...", "...has been demolished and replaced by...", "...diverting traffic away from...". Prepositions of place are consistently accurate. Vague "got bigger / got smaller" phrasing would cap LR at 6.
  • GRA: Band 8 — present perfect for change ("has been demolished", "have been converted", "has been reduced") contrasted with past simple for the 2000 state ("occupied", "lay", "passed"), plus concession ("although the station itself is unchanged"). Majority error-free.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: has/have been + past participle + and + replaced/converted + into/by — example: "the market has been demolished and replaced by a covered shopping mall". Job: expresses removal and substitution as one completed change.
  • Frame: present-participle purpose clause after a new feature — example: "A ring road now circles the centre, diverting traffic away from Station Road". Job: adds function to location, which is what separates band 8 map answers from inventories.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Paraphrase: place + two time points.
  2. Overview: overall character of change + what stayed the same.
  3. Past layout: centre, then north/east/south anchors with road pattern.
  4. Present layout: same locations revisited in the same order, each old–new paired.

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Sketch the 2000 layout from the paragraph-3 location phrases alone, then write two sentences describing your own street's changes using "replaced by" and "converted into".
Do not copy: "diverting traffic away from Station Road, which is pedestrianised" — works only here because it depends on Millford's invented road names and layout.
