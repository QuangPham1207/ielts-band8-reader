W-T1-04 — PROCESS: HOW RECYCLED PAPER IS MADE

Writing Task 1 · Process diagram · ~170 words · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. The diagram below illustrates the six-stage process by which used paper (newspapers, cardboard, office paper) is converted into new recycled paper rolls: collection and transport, sorting and contaminant removal, pulping with water and chemicals, de-inking, sheet formation on a mesh, and pressing, drying and rolling. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features. Write at least 150 words. Time target: 20 minutes.

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

The diagram illustrates the stages involved in manufacturing recycled paper from used paper products.

Overall, the process consists of six main steps, beginning with the collection of waste paper and ending with finished rolls ready for distribution. Water and mechanical treatment are central throughout, transforming loose paper back into a uniform sheet.

First, discarded newspapers, cardboard and office paper are collected and transported to a recycling plant, where they are sorted by grade and contaminants such as staples and plastic wrapping are removed. The sorted paper is then mixed with water and chemicals in a large vat, where rotating blades break it down into a slurry known as pulp.

Next, the pulp is washed and passed through a de-inking stage, in which ink and small impurities are separated by air bubbles and skimmed off. The cleaned pulp is subsequently spread onto a moving mesh, where excess water drains away and fibres begin to bond. Finally, the damp sheet is pressed between heated rollers, dried thoroughly and wound into large rolls.

2. Why it scores

  • TA: Band 8 — all six stages covered with clear stage selection: collection, sorting, pulping, de-inking, forming, finishing. The overview counts the steps and names the start, end and governing principle (water + mechanical treatment). Omitting de-inking or the final rolling would cap TA at 6–7 for missing key stages.
  • CC: Band 8 — sequencing without mechanical chains: time markers ("First", "Next", "Finally", "subsequently", "then") are spaced one per stage and carried mainly by relative clauses ("where they are sorted", "in which ink ... are separated"). Stacking "Moreover / Furthermore" between steps would cap CC at 6.
  • LR: Band 8 — process-precise lexis: "...contaminants such as staples...", "...a slurry known as pulp...", "...excess water drains away...". Each term names the exact material or action. A memorised academic filler ("in today's modern society") would cap LR at 6–7 as irrelevant.
  • GRA: Band 8 — passive range dominates appropriately ("are collected", "is mixed", "are separated", "is pressed ... dried ... and wound"), varied with active relatives ("where rotating blades break it down", "where excess water drains away"). Majority error-free.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: X is + past participle + where/in which + passive detail — example: "The sorted paper is then mixed with water and chemicals in a large vat, where rotating blades break it down". Job: states the step and its mechanism in one sentence, avoiding two short steps.
  • Frame: present-participle overview pair — example: "beginning with the collection of waste paper and ending with finished rolls". Job: compresses the whole process arc into the overview without listing stages.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Paraphrase: input material + output product.
  2. Overview: number of steps + start + end + governing principle.
  3. Stages 1–3: collection/sorting/pulping grouped (preparation of raw material).
  4. Stages 4–6: de-inking/forming/finishing grouped (cleaning and rebuilding the sheet).

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Retell stages 4–6 from memory using only passives, then check each verb has a correct agent or no agent where none is needed.
Do not copy: "a slurry known as pulp" — works only here because "pulp" is the technical term of this paper process, not a general-purpose phrase.
