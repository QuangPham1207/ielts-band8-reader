W-T1-03 — TABLE: AVERAGE MONTHLY STUDENT SPENDING IN THREE CITIES

Writing Task 1 · Table · ~169 words · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. The table below shows average monthly student spending, in euros, on rent, food, transport and leisure in three fictional cities: Bellfield, Kesterton and Lornwick. Rent is the largest item everywhere (520–680 euros); total spending is highest in Lornwick (about 1,065 euros) and lowest in Bellfield; leisure is highest in Bellfield (140 euros) and lowest in Lornwick (85 euros); transport is 55–70 euros. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words. Time target: 20 minutes.

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

The table compares average monthly student expenditure, in euros, on four categories — rent, food, transport and leisure — in the cities of Bellfield, Kesterton and Lornwick.

Overall, rent accounted for the largest share of spending in all three cities, and total outgoings were highest in Lornwick. Transport varied the least between cities, while leisure spending differed the most in proportional terms.

Monthly rent stood at 520 euros in Bellfield, 610 euros in Kesterton and 680 euros in Lornwick, representing roughly half of each city's total. Food costs followed a similar order, rising from 210 euros in Bellfield to 245 euros in Lornwick.

By contrast, students in Bellfield spent the most on leisure, at 140 euros, compared with only 85 euros in Lornwick, where the high cost of housing appeared to squeeze discretionary budgets. Transport costs were modest in all three cities, at only 55 to 70 euros per month. In total, a student in Lornwick spent around 1,065 euros a month, about 190 euros more than in Bellfield.

2. Why it scores

  • TA: Band 8 — dense table reduced to key features: biggest item (rent), highest total (Lornwick), least/most variable items (transport vs. leisure). No row is copied in full. Transcribing all twelve cells would cap TA at 6 for presenting details without selection.
  • CC: Band 8 — grouping by pattern, not by table order: essentials together (rent + food), exceptions together (leisure reversal + transport). "By contrast" is earned by a genuine reversal. A template "Firstly / Secondly / Thirdly" walk through rows would cap CC at 6–7 as mechanical.
  • LR: Band 8 — precise financial lexis: "...accounted for the largest share...", "...total outgoings...", "...squeeze discretionary budgets". "Outgoings" and "discretionary" are uncommon items used accurately. Forcing in "highly contentious" style memorised lexis would cap LR at 6–7.
  • GRA: Band 8 — relative clause with causal reading ("...where the high cost of housing appeared to squeeze..."), participial phrase ("...representing roughly half..."), and hedged comparison ("...about 190 euros more than..."). Majority error-free with consistent article control.

3. Stealable language

accounted for the largest share of spendingcollocationStates dominance as a share, which survives even when totals differ across cities.
total outgoings were highest in Lornwicknoun phrase"Outgoings" is the natural uncommon noun for summed expenditure; reuse for any budget table.
varied the least / differed the most in proportional termscomparison pairCompares variability itself — the key move when a table has too many numbers to list.

squeeze discretionary budgetscollocationExplains the leisure reversal plausibly; "discretionary" collocates with budgets/spending, not with rent.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: X, representing roughly + fraction + of ... — example: "representing roughly half of each city's total". Job: adds interpretation to raw figures without a new sentence.
  • Frame: where + cause-effect relative clause — example: "where the high cost of housing appeared to squeeze discretionary budgets". Job: links two table facts (high rent, low leisure) into one explanatory relationship, hedged with "appeared".

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Paraphrase: unit, categories, cities.
  2. Overview: largest item + highest total + most/least variable items, no numbers.
  3. Detail A: items that follow the same city order (rent, food) with selective figures.
  4. Detail B: items that break the order (leisure reversal, flat transport) + total comparison.

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Rebuild the overview using no numbers: name the biggest category, the costliest city, and which category varies most and least.
Do not copy: "about 190 euros more than in Bellfield" — works only here because it depends on this table's invented totals.
