S-P1-04 — Food & Cooking

Speaking Part 1 · 5 questions · answers of 2–3 sentences · original questions, not from any past paper

Prompt. Answer each question in 2–3 sentences: a direct answer plus ONE extension (a reason, detail, contrast, or past–future comparison). Aim for about 20–30 seconds per answer. Questions: 1. What is your favourite meal of the day? 2. Do you enjoy cooking? 3. How often do you eat out? 4. Has what you eat changed since childhood? 5. Is there a dish you would like to learn to make?

1. Sample answers (band 8+)

1. What is your favourite meal of the day?
Dinner, without question. It is the only meal I never rush, so I actually taste what I am eating instead of scrolling through my phone.

2. Do you enjoy cooking?
Yes, but only simple things — stir-fries, soups, that kind of thing. Anything with precise measurements tends to go wrong when I am in a hurry.

3. How often do you eat out?
Maybe twice a month, usually with friends at a small noodle place near my flat. Restaurant prices have climbed so much that cooking at home feels like the sensible default now.

4. Has what you eat changed since childhood?
Definitely. I grew up on quite plain food because my parents kept things simple, whereas now I actively look for stronger flavours like chilli and herbs.

5. Is there a dish you would like to learn to make?
Proper hand-pulled noodles. I have watched videos of chefs stretching the dough, and it looks like the kind of skill that takes months of practice to get right.

2. Why it scores

  • Fluency & Coherence (band 8 logic: answers are fully extended but stay concise and relevant): "Dinner, without question" answers in two words, then one reason-detail extension. No circling back, no second unprompted idea.
  • Lexical Resource (band 8 logic: natural less-common items used precisely): "quite plain food", "stronger flavours", "the sensible default", "hand-pulled noodles". Food-domain precision rather than generic "delicious" repetition.
  • Grammatical Range & Accuracy (band 8 logic: mixes simple and complex with good control): "Anything with precise measurements tends to go wrong when…" (generic reference + time clause), "I grew up on… whereas now I…" (past–present contrast), present perfect ("prices have climbed", "I have watched").
  • Pronunciation — text-only notes (cannot be judged from text): on paper, only targets can be noted. In "quite plain food because my parents kept things simple", keep the /p/–/b/ contrast clear (plain vs parents) and de-stress "because" to /bɪkəz/; in "Dinner, without question", a short pause after "Dinner" gives the short answer punch. Practise chunking Q1 as two beats, not five words in a row.

On memorised language: dropping a learnt "food is an indispensable part of our lives" opener here would cap Lexical Resource (inflated, off-register phrasing for a simple Part 1 question) and Coherence (delays the direct answer). The scoring move is answering the exact question first.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: "It is the only + noun + (relative clause)" — example: "It is the only meal I never rush". Job: justifies a "favourite" pick with exclusivity in a single clause.
  • Frame: "I grew up on X, whereas now I Y" — example: "I grew up on quite plain food…, whereas now I actively look for stronger flavours". Job: carries any childhood-vs-now comparison with one clean pivot.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Q1: pick one meal + why it stands out
  2. Q2: yes/no with limit + what goes wrong or right
  3. Q3: frequency + where/with whom + reason
  4. Q4: childhood diet + now-contrast
  5. Q5: target dish + why it attracts you (skill, memory, taste)

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Answer Q2–Q4 about your own real eating habits using only the outline. Keep each answer under 30 seconds and include one past–present "whereas" contrast.
Do not copy: "Usually with friends at a small noodle place near my flat." — works only here because the frequency, company and place details belong to this speaker; reused elsewhere it would cap Coherence as an unanchored detail.
