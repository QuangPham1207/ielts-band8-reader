S-P1-05 — Weather & Short Trips

Speaking Part 1 · 5 questions · answers of 2–3 sentences · original questions, not from any past paper

Prompt. Answer each question in 2–3 sentences: a direct answer plus ONE extension (a reason, detail, contrast, or past–future comparison). Aim for about 20–30 seconds per answer. Questions: 1. What is the weather usually like where you live? 2. What is your favourite season? 3. Did you take any short trips recently? 4. What would your ideal weekend trip look like? 5. Do you prefer travelling with others or on your own?

1. Sample answers (band 8+)

1. What is the weather usually like where you live?
Fairly mild for most of the year, with a long damp spring and a short sharp burst of heat in summer. We rarely get extremes, which suits me fine since I cycle everywhere.

2. What is your favourite season?
Autumn, mainly because the light turns golden and the streets are suddenly quieter. It feels like the city exhales after the crowded summer months.

3. Did you take any short trips recently?
Yes — last month I spent a weekend in a small lakeside town about three hours away by bus. We mostly walked along the shore and ate far too much, which was exactly the point.

4. What would your ideal weekend trip look like?
Somewhere coastal but off-season, so the paths are empty and guesthouses are cheap. I would spend the mornings hiking and the afternoons reading somewhere with a view of the water.

5. Do you prefer travelling with others or on your own?
With one or two close friends, ideally. Solo trips give me freedom, but shared ones leave me with better stories — and someone to split the driving with.

2. Why it scores

  • Fluency & Coherence (band 8 logic: develops topics coherently with a range of connectives used naturally): "Somewhere coastal but off-season, so the paths are empty and guesthouses are cheap" builds the ideal trip from one condition to two consequences. Causes and results stay adjacent — no backtracking.
  • Lexical Resource (band 8 logic: wide, precise, natural): weather precision in "long damp spring" and "a short sharp burst of heat"; travel precision in "off-season", "guesthouses", "split the driving". Adjective–noun pairings do the descriptive work.
  • Grammatical Range & Accuracy (band 8 logic: flexible use of structures, mostly error-free): present habits ("I cycle everywhere"), past narrative ("I spent a weekend…"), hypothetical ("I would spend the mornings…"), concession ("Solo trips give me freedom, but…"). Four jobs across five answers.
  • Pronunciation — text-only notes (cannot be judged from text): on paper, only targets can be noted. In "a short sharp burst of heat", the /ʃ/ cluster (short, sharp) needs clean separation, not blending; in "the city exhales after the crowded summer months", sentence stress on "ex-HALES" carries the metaphor. Rehearse Q2 aloud and let the voice drop on "quieter" for the seasonal contrast.

On memorised language: a pre-learnt "weather plays a pivotal role in our daily lives" sentence would cap Lexical Resource (pompous collocation for a simple weather question) and Coherence (general claim instead of describing this place). The sample names actual weather — transfer that specificity.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: "Somewhere + adjective, so + result(s)" — example: "Somewhere coastal but off-season, so the paths are empty and guesthouses are cheap". Job: defines an ideal by condition-then-payoff, perfect for "ideal trip" questions.
  • Frame: "X give(s) me A, but Y…" — example: "Solo trips give me freedom, but shared ones leave me with better stories". Job: weighs two options in one balanced sentence without a memorised linker chain.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Q1: general pattern + seasonal detail + personal effect
  2. Q2: pick one season + sensory reason + atmosphere contrast
  3. Q3: when + where + what you actually did
  4. Q4: place condition + morning/afternoon split
  5. Q5: preference + concession to the other side + practical reason

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Answer Q1–Q3 about your real location and last real trip using only the outline. Include one hypothetical ("I would…") and one past narrative ("last month I…"), each under 30 seconds.
Do not copy: "Last month I spent a weekend in a small lakeside town about three hours away by bus." — works only here because the time, place and transport details are this speaker's story; transplanted, it would cap Coherence as an uncheckable invention under follow-up questions.
