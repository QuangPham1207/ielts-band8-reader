S-P1-01 — Work & Studies

Speaking Part 1 · 6 questions · answers of 2–3 sentences · original questions, not from any past paper

Prompt. Answer each question in 2–3 sentences: a direct answer plus ONE extension (a reason, detail, contrast, or past–future comparison). Aim for about 20–30 seconds per answer. Questions: 1. Do you work or are you a student? 2. What subject are you studying / what does your job involve? 3. What do you enjoy most about it? 4. What does a typical day look like for you? 5. Is there anything you would like to change about your routine? 6. What are your plans for the next year or two?

1. Sample answers (band 8+)

1. Do you work or are you a student?
At the moment I am working full-time as a junior marketing assistant for a small clothing brand. I graduated last year, so I am still learning the ropes, which keeps things interesting.

2. What does your job involve?
Mostly I handle our social media posts and track how each campaign performs. It is quite data-driven, so I spend a lot of time comparing figures and adjusting the schedule for the following week.

3. What do you enjoy most about it?
I would say the creative side, especially coming up with short video ideas with the design team. Seeing a post we planned together actually get shared feels really rewarding.

4. What does a typical day look like for you?
I usually start around nine, check messages, and then block out the morning for focused work before meetings pile up. Evenings are quieter, so I often use that time to review what worked during the day.

5. Is there anything you would like to change about your routine?
I wish I had a shorter commute, since the bus journey takes nearly an hour each way. If I could work from home even one day a week, I would have far more time to read and exercise.

6. What are your plans for the next year or two?
I am hoping to take on more responsibility for whole campaigns rather than just single posts. In the longer term I might do a short course in data analysis, because that skill seems increasingly valuable in my field.

2. Why it scores

  • Fluency & Coherence (band 8 logic: develops each answer without noticeable effort, extends the topic coherently): every answer follows direct-answer-plus-one-extension, e.g. "I graduated last year, so I am still learning the ropes, which keeps things interesting." The extension is signalled by natural logic ("so", "since", "If I could") rather than stacked linkers.
  • Lexical Resource (band 8 logic: wide, precise resource with skilful less-common items, occasional slight inaccuracy allowed): topic-precise choices like "learning the ropes", "quite data-driven", "block out the morning", "meetings pile up". Each fits this work context rather than decorating any topic.
  • Grammatical Range & Accuracy (band 8 logic: wide range of structures, majority of sentences error-free): tense range across the set — present continuous ("I am working"), present perfect ("I have" implied in "I graduated last year, so I am still…"), second conditional ("If I could work from home… I would have"), and relative clauses ("a post we planned together").
  • Pronunciation — text-only notes (cannot be judged from text): on paper, only the features to aim for can be noted. In "comparing figures and adjusting the schedule", keep unstressed syllables weak and link "figures‿and" smoothly; in "If I could work from home", let the voice fall on "home" to mark the condition clearly. Record and check for even chunking rather than word-by-word delivery.

On memorised language: delivering these sentences from memory would cap Fluency & Coherence (recitation rhythm and self-correction in the wrong places signal a learnt turn) and Lexical Resource (phrases like "learning the ropes" stop counting as flexible use when they appear regardless of the question). Reuse the extension move, not the sentence.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: "I + present continuous + time phrase, + so + present state" — example: "I am working full-time…, so I am still learning the ropes". Job: states a current situation and draws the natural consequence in one move.
  • Frame: "If I could + verb…, I would + result" — example: "If I could work from home even one day a week, I would have far more time". Job: turns a complaint (long commute) into a controlled conditional wish, showing hypothetical grammar.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Q1: current status + when started / how it feels
  2. Q2: two concrete duties + what kind of work that makes it
  3. Q3: pick one favourite aspect + evidence moment
  4. Q4: morning pattern + evening contrast
  5. Q5: one change wished for + conditional benefit
  6. Q6: near-term step + longer-term option with reason

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Retell answers 2, 4 and 5 from the outline alone, changing every content word you can (different job, different hours) but keeping the two grammar frames above. Time yourself: max 30 seconds per answer.
Do not copy: "Mostly I handle our social media posts and track how each campaign performs." — works only here because the social-media detail answers this exact job question; pasted into another routine or study answer it would cap Task Response for irrelevance.
