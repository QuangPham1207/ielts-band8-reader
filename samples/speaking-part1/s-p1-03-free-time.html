S-P1-03 — Free Time & Evenings

Speaking Part 1 · 5 questions · answers of 2–3 sentences · original questions, not from any past paper

Prompt. Answer each question in 2–3 sentences: a direct answer plus ONE extension (a reason, detail, contrast, or past–future comparison). Aim for about 20–30 seconds per answer. Questions: 1. How do you usually spend your free time? 2. Who do you spend your evenings with? 3. Has your free time changed compared with your childhood? 4. What do you usually do at weekends? 5. Would you like more free time?

1. Sample answers (band 8+)

1. How do you usually spend your free time?
Mostly I go for long walks while listening to podcasts, which helps me switch off after work. If the weather is bad, I will cook something slow like soup and read for an hour or two.

2. Who do you spend your evenings with?
Usually just my flatmate — we take turns making dinner and then watch an episode of something. It is low-key, but after a busy day that is exactly what I need.

3. Has your free time changed compared with your childhood?
Quite a lot. As a child I played outside with neighbours almost every afternoon, whereas now my free hours are shorter and mostly indoors.

4. What do you usually do at weekends?
Saturdays I try to get out of the city, even if it is only a short train ride to the hills. Sundays are deliberately quiet: laundry, a bit of planning, and an early night.

5. Would you like more free time?
Of course, though I suspect I would just fill it with more of the same. What I really need is not more hours but fewer distractions inside the hours I already have.

2. Why it scores

  • Fluency & Coherence (band 8 logic: sustains each answer, links ideas with a range of markers used naturally): "Saturdays I try to get out…, Sundays are deliberately quiet" organises the weekend answer by time contrast with zero filler. Each answer closes after one extension — no trailing.
  • Lexical Resource (band 8 logic: flexible, natural, topic-fitting less-common items): "switch off after work", "take turns making dinner", "deliberately quiet", "fewer distractions". Spoken register, not essay vocabulary forced into speech.
  • Grammatical Range & Accuracy (band 8 logic: variety of complex forms with good control): conditional ("If the weather is bad, I will…"), past-vs-present contrast ("As a child I played…, whereas now…"), and cleft-like focus ("What I really need is not more hours but…").
  • Pronunciation — text-only notes (cannot be judged from text): on paper, only targets can be noted. In "take turns making dinner", link "turns‿making" without inserting a vowel; in "not more hours but fewer distractions", contrastive stress on "NOT… BUT" carries the meaning. Practise the Q5 sentence as two chunks with a clear pause before "but".

On memorised language: opening any answer with a learnt filler ("That is an interesting question…") would cap Fluency & Coherence (stalling dressed as discourse) and irritate under Lexical Resource (empty prefabricated language). The samples start directly — copy that habit, not the words.

3. Stealable language

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: "If + present simple, I will + habit" — example: "If the weather is bad, I will cook something slow". Job: gives a conditional routine (not a one-off plan), showing first-conditional control inside a habit answer.
  • Frame: "What I really need is not X but Y" — example: "What I really need is not more hours but fewer distractions". Job: corrects the question's assumption elegantly and delivers the opinion in one clause.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Q1: main habit + purpose; bad-weather alternative
  2. Q2: who + shared routine + why it suits you
  3. Q3: childhood pattern + now-pattern contrast
  4. Q4: Saturday (active) vs Sunday (quiet) split
  5. Q5: concede yes + redirect to the real point

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Retell Q1, Q3 and Q5 with different content (different hobby, different childhood memory) but keep one conditional and one "not X but Y" correction. Record once and cut any opener before the direct answer.
Do not copy: "Usually just my flatmate — we take turns making dinner and then watch an episode of something." — works only here because the flatmate setup was the speaker's real situation; borrowed into another living arrangement it would cap Coherence for invented inconsistency.
