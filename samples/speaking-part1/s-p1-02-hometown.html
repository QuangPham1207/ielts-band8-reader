S-P1-02 — Hometown & Neighbourhood

Speaking Part 1 · 5 questions · answers of 2–3 sentences · original questions, not from any past paper

Prompt. Answer each question in 2–3 sentences: a direct answer plus ONE extension (a reason, detail, contrast, or past–future comparison). Aim for about 20–30 seconds per answer. Questions: 1. Where is your hometown? 2. What is it known for? 3. How has it changed in recent years? 4. What do you like or dislike about living there? 5. Do you know your neighbours well?

1. Sample answers (band 8+)

1. Where is your hometown?
I come from a mid-sized coastal city in the south, about two hours by train from the capital. It is not particularly famous, but it has a busy fishing port that shapes daily life there.

2. What is it known for?
Mostly its seafood and the old market streets near the harbour. Visitors often come just to walk around that area and try the grilled fish, which is genuinely excellent.

3. How has it changed in recent years?
The biggest change has been the new waterfront promenade, which replaced a fairly run-down stretch of warehouses. It used to feel quite empty in the evenings, whereas now families gather there after dinner.

4. What do you like or dislike about living there?
I like how compact everything is — I can cycle to most places within fifteen minutes. The downside is the traffic noise near the main road, which gets tiring when you are trying to sleep.

5. Do you know your neighbours well?
Fairly well, actually. We exchange small favours like collecting parcels, and during holidays we usually share food, so there is a quiet sense of trust on our floor.

2. Why it scores

  • Fluency & Coherence (band 8 logic: speaks at length with little hesitation, develops topics fully and logically): each answer stays on the question and extends once, e.g. "It used to feel quite empty…, whereas now families gather there" — a clean past–present contrast with no filler.
  • Lexical Resource (band 8 logic: wide resource, precise and natural less-common items): place-description precision in "mid-sized coastal city", "fairly run-down stretch of warehouses", "waterfront promenade". Each item names something concrete about this town.
  • Grammatical Range & Accuracy (band 8 logic: wide range, majority error-free): "which replaced…" and "which gets tiring…" (relative clauses), "It used to feel…" (past habit) against present simple, present perfect ("The biggest change has been…"). Range is spread across answers, not crammed into one.
  • Pronunciation — text-only notes (cannot be judged from text): on paper, only targets can be noted. In "fairly run-down stretch of warehouses", stress the content words (run-DOWN, STRETCH, WAREhouses) and weaken "of" to /əv/; in "whereas now families gather there", pause lightly before "whereas" so the contrast lands. Record and check the contrast is audible, not flat.

On memorised language: reciting a pre-written hometown paragraph regardless of the question would cap Coherence (the answer drifts from "neighbours" to generic city praise) and Lexical Resource ("waterfront promenade" counts only when this town actually has one). Transfer the contrast move, not the place details.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: "It used to + verb…, whereas now + present simple" — example: "It used to feel quite empty…, whereas now families gather there". Job: compresses change-over-time into one sentence, ideal for "how has it changed" questions.
  • Frame: "The downside is + noun phrase, which + effect" — example: "The downside is the traffic noise…, which gets tiring…". Job: balances a positive with a precise negative without a memorised "on the other hand" pivot.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Q1: location in relation to somewhere known + one defining feature
  2. Q2: two things known for + who they attract / why
  3. Q3: biggest change + before-vs-now contrast
  4. Q4: one like (with measurable detail) + one dislike (with effect)
  5. Q5: how well + one concrete example of contact

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Answer all five questions about a different place (real or invented) using only the outline. Keep the "used to…, whereas now" frame for Q3 and the "downside" frame for Q4, with entirely new details.
Do not copy: "Visitors often come just to walk around that area and try the grilled fish, which is genuinely excellent." — works only here because the harbour-and-seafood context was built in Q1–Q2; transplanted to another town it would cap Coherence for unsupported detail.
