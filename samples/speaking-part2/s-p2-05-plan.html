S-P2-05 — SOMETHING YOU WANT TO LEARN IN THE FUTURE

Speaking Part 2 · Plan cue card · ~205 words · ~1.5–2 min · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. Describe something you want to learn in the future. You should say: what it is — how you plan to learn it — why you want to learn it — and explain how it might change your life. (Original cue card; you have one minute to prepare and should speak for 1–2 minutes.)

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

I would like to talk about photography, which I am planning to learn properly next year once my work schedule settles down. I have always enjoyed looking at other people's photos, but I have never gone beyond taking quick snaps on my phone.

My first step will be joining a weekend course at a local arts centre, where I will learn how to use a proper camera and understand things like light and composition. After that, I am going to save up for a second-hand camera and practise regularly — probably by spending Sunday mornings in parks or old streets, building up a small collection. I am also hoping to find an online community where I can share my attempts and get honest feedback, since learning alone feels slower.

The main reason is that I want a hobby that gets me outside and forces me to notice details I would normally walk past. I am also thinking ahead: in a few years I will be travelling more, and I would love to capture those trips properly rather than with blurry phone shots. If I stick with it, I might even print my favourites and hang them at home, which would feel like a real achievement. It seems like the kind of skill that keeps rewarding patience.

2. Why it scores

  • TR: All bullets answered concretely: what ("photography"), how (course, saving for a camera, Sunday practice, online community), why ("a hobby that gets me outside", "travelling more"), and future effect ("capture those trips properly", "print my favourites"). Band 8 logic: all parts sufficiently addressed with extended, supported ideas. Wishing without a plan ("I want to learn it someday") would leave the "how" bullet empty and cap TR.
  • CC: Clear staged plan — "My first step will be… After that… I am also hoping…" — followed by reasons ordered from present hobby to future travel. Each stage earns its linker through real sequence. Band 8 logic: logically sequenced, cohesion well managed. A memorised "Moreover/Furthermore/In conclusion" chain over this plan would cap CC as mechanical.
  • LR: Future-plan vocabulary is varied and fitted: "my work schedule settles down", "quick snaps", "save up for a second-hand camera", "building up a small collection", "honest feedback", "blurry phone shots". "Snaps" and "second-hand" are the skilful natural choices a memorised synonym list would miss. Band 8 logic: flexible range, skilful uncommon use. A pre-learned "broaden my horizons" closer would cap LR as unfitted.
  • GRA: Future forms are ranged, not repeated: "will be joining", "will learn", "am going to save up", "am planning to learn", "am hoping to find", "would love to capture", "might even print", plus a first conditional ("If I stick with it…"). Majority error-free. Band 8 logic: wide range with good control. Repeating only "will" through a future plan is the classic range limit; variety here avoids it.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: Staged future sequence ("My first step will be… After that, I am going to… I am also hoping to…") — example: "After that, I am going to save up for a second-hand camera and practise regularly". Job: lays out a believable multi-step plan; reuse for any future-plan cue.
  • Frame: First conditional payoff ("If I stick with it, I might even…") — example: "If I stick with it, I might even print my favourites and hang them at home". Job: answers "how it might change your life" without overclaiming; "might" keeps a future benefit honest.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Orient: name the skill + when you plan to start + current level.
  2. Beat 1: staged plan — course or teacher, equipment, practice routine.
  3. Beat 2: support — community, feedback, or study partner.
  4. Reflect: why — present reason + future payoff ("if I stick with it…").

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Retell this monologue from the outline only about a skill you genuinely want to learn. Keep the three-stage plan and the "If I stick with it" conditional ending with your own steps.
Do not copy: "probably by spending Sunday mornings in parks or old streets, building up a small collection" — works only here because it is the practice routine for this exact photography plan; copying it caps TR/LR as content that answers a different prompt.
