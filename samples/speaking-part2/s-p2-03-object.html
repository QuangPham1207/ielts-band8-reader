S-P2-03 — SOMETHING YOU REPAIRED OR MADE BY HAND

Speaking Part 2 · Object cue card · ~210 words · ~1.5–2 min · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. Describe something you repaired or made by hand. You should say: what it was — why you made or repaired it — how you did it — and explain how you felt about the result. (Original cue card; you have one minute to prepare and should speak for 1–2 minutes.)

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

I am going to talk about a wooden bookshelf I built last year when I moved into a smaller flat and could not find anything that fitted the corner by my desk. I had never made furniture before, but a friend lent me his tools and sent me a simple design.

I started by measuring the space carefully and cutting the pine boards to size in the building's basement garage, which took far longer than I expected because I kept double-checking every number. Then I sanded each piece smooth, fixed the sides to the shelves with screws and wood glue, and left the whole frame clamped overnight so the joints would hold. The trickiest part was getting it level — I had to sand down one leg twice before it stopped wobbling.

Painting it a plain dark grey took another weekend, but when I finally carried it upstairs and loaded it with books, it looked far better than anything I had seen in the shops. What made it satisfying was not saving money but knowing every uneven edge was mine. It still wobbles slightly if I push it, which honestly makes me like it more. Friends always assume I bought it, and I enjoy telling them I built it myself with borrowed tools and a lot of patience.

2. Why it scores

  • TR: All bullets covered inside a real story: what ("a wooden bookshelf"), why ("could not find anything that fitted the corner"), how (measuring, cutting, sanding, clamping, painting — a full sequenced process), and feeling ("What made it satisfying was…"). Band 8 logic: all parts sufficiently addressed with extended, supported ideas. A process list with no reason and no feeling would leave two bullets thin and cap TR.
  • CC: The process unfolds chronologically — "I started by… Then… left… overnight… Painting it… took another weekend" — with time markers growing out of the story rather than numbered steps. The wobble detail returns at the end, closing the loop. Band 8 logic: logical sequencing, cohesion well managed. Numbered-linker chains ("Firstly, moreover, furthermore") would sound mechanical and cap CC.
  • LR: Workshop vocabulary is exact and earned: "cutting the pine boards to size", "sanded each piece smooth", "left the whole frame clamped overnight", "so the joints would hold". Each term does real descriptive work for this object. Band 8 logic: flexible range with skilful uncommon items. Memorised "handywork" phrases applied to the wrong material or tool would cap LR through collocation error.
  • GRA: Sequenced past narration with purpose clauses ("so the joints would hold", "before it stopped wobbling") and a cleft-style evaluation ("What made it satisfying was not… but…"). Majority of sentences error-free across simple and complex forms. Band 8 logic: wide range with good control. Forced memorised frames usually break verb forms in process descriptions; control holds here.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: Sequenced past + reason clause ("…which took far longer than I expected because I kept…") — example: "which took far longer than I expected because I kept double-checking every number". Job: narrates a step and explains the delay in one sentence; reuse for any process-inside-a-story.
  • Frame: "What made it ADJ was not X but Y" — example: "What made it satisfying was not saving money but knowing every uneven edge was mine." Job: delivers the feeling bullet with contrast and weight; reuse for any "how did you feel" ending.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Orient: name the object + why you made/repaired it + your starting skill level.
  2. Beat 1: the process in 3–4 sequenced steps with one technical detail.
  3. Beat 2: the hardest moment + how you fixed it.
  4. Reflect: the result, how it looks/feels now, and why it matters.

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Retell this monologue from the outline only about something you really fixed or made (a meal, a repair, a school project). Keep the 3-step process sequence and the " trickiest part" sentence with your own difficulty.
Do not copy: "left the whole frame clamped overnight so the joints would hold" — works only here because it describes this exact glued bookshelf; transplanting it to another object would cap LR as an unfitted memorised detail.
