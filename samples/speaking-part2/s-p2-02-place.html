S-P2-02 — A QUIET PLACE YOU LIKE

Speaking Part 2 · Place cue card · ~205 words · ~1.5–2 min · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. Describe a quiet place you like to go. You should say: where it is — when you go there — what you do there — and explain why you like it. (Original cue card; you have one minute to prepare and should speak for 1–2 minutes.)

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

I want to describe a small reading room on the top floor of our city library, where I go whenever I need real quiet. I usually visit on Saturday mornings, arriving early before the crowds, and I often stay for two or three hours.

The first thing you notice is the sound — or rather the absence of it. After the traffic and motorbike horns outside, the room feels almost unnaturally still, with only pages turning and the faint hum of the air conditioning. Tall windows let in soft natural light, and from my corner seat I can see the treetops moving slightly in the wind. I usually bring a novel or some study notes, but honestly half the pleasure is just sitting there with a cup of tea, thinking without being interrupted.

What I value most is the contrast with the streets below. My neighbourhood is noisy from six in the morning, with construction work and vendors shouting, so this room feels like a different city entirely. Every time I leave, I notice I walk more slowly and feel calmer for the rest of the day. That sense of reset is why I keep going back. It has become my favourite way to start a busy weekend.

2. Why it scores

  • TR: Every bullet is answered: where ("reading room on the top floor of our city library"), when ("Saturday mornings"), what ("bring a novel or some study notes", "sitting there with a cup of tea"), and why ("That sense of reset is why I keep going back"). Band 8 logic: all parts sufficiently addressed with extended ideas. A generic "beautiful place" description that never said when or what the speaker does there would cap TR.
  • CC: The talk moves in a clear arc — orientation, then sensory detail, then contrast and reflection — linked by earned transitions ("After the traffic…", "What I value most…") rather than listed linkers. Band 8 logic: logically sequenced, cohesion well managed. A memorised "Moreover/Furthermore" chain between unrelated sentences would cap CC as mechanical.
  • LR: Sensory vocabulary is precise and fitted: "the absence of it", "almost unnaturally still", "the faint hum of the air conditioning", "soft natural light". "Unnaturally" is the skilful uncommon choice — surprising but exact after street noise. Band 8 logic: flexible range, skilful uncommon use. A pre-learned "picturesque scenery" phrase dropped in regardless of this indoor setting would cap LR as a collocation error.
  • GRA: Present habits ("I usually visit", "I usually bring"), contrast clauses ("After the traffic… the room feels…"), and reduced participle phrases ("arriving early before the crowds", "thinking without being interrupted") combine without errors. Band 8 logic: wide range, majority of sentences error-free. Memorised complex frames tend to break article and verb-form control; none intrudes here.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: "After X, Y feels…" — example: "After the traffic and motorbike horns outside, the room feels almost unnaturally still." Job: builds contrast in one sentence; reuse for any place defined against its surroundings.
  • Frame: Participle opener for habit ("arriving early…", "thinking without being interrupted") — example: "arriving early before the crowds". Job: packs routine detail without a new clause; reuse to describe repeated visits compactly.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Orient: name the place + where + when you go (cover where/when fast).
  2. Beat 1: sensory detail — one sound, one sight, what you do.
  3. Beat 2: contrast with the noisy world outside.
  4. Reflect: the effect on you + why you return.

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Retell this monologue from the outline only about a real quiet spot you know (a park bench, a café corner, a relative's house). Keep the "one small sound" detail and the "After X, it feels…" contrast frame with your own content.
Do not copy: "with only pages turning and the faint hum of the air conditioning" — works only here because it is the true sound of this exact library room; copying it into another place answer would cap LR as an unfitted memorised chunk.
