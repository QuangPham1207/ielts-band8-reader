S-P2-04 — A TIME YOU ARRIVED LATE OR WERE STUCK

Speaking Part 2 · Event cue card · ~210 words · ~1.5–2 min · original prompt, not from any past paper

Prompt. Describe a time when you arrived late or were stuck somewhere. You should say: what happened — why you were late or stuck — what you did — and explain what you learned from the experience. (Original cue card; you have one minute to prepare and should speak for 1–2 minutes.)

1. Sample answer (band 8+)

I will describe a morning last winter when I got stuck in a traffic jam and arrived almost an hour late for an important job interview. I had planned everything carefully, leaving the house ninety minutes early, but halfway there the bus simply stopped moving behind a long line of cars.

At first I stayed calm, assuming it was a short delay, but after twenty minutes without moving I started checking the time constantly and messaging a friend to warn the company. When the driver announced that an accident had blocked the road ahead, my stomach dropped — there was no side street to escape through, so all I could do was sit there watching the minutes pass. I eventually jumped out and ran the last kilometre in my formal shoes, arriving sweaty and breathless while the interviewers were already waiting.

Surprisingly, they were understanding once I explained, and the interview went better than I had feared. Looking back, that morning taught me to always have a backup route and to contact people early rather than hoping the problem disappears. I still leave extra time for anything important, because the memory of sitting helplessly in that bus has never quite left me. It was an uncomfortable lesson, but probably more useful than the interview itself.

2. Why it scores

  • TR: All bullets answered with a complete event: what ("stuck in a traffic jam… an hour late for an important job interview"), why ("an accident had blocked the road"), what was done ("jumped out and ran the last kilometre"), and the lesson ("taught me to always have a backup route"). Band 8 logic: all parts sufficiently addressed, ideas extended and supported. Stopping at the drama without the "what you learned" reflection would cap TR.
  • CC: Tension rises in order — calm, checking the time, the driver's announcement, the run — marked by time language ("At first… after twenty minutes… eventually… Looking back") that tracks the speaker's feelings, not a template list. Band 8 logic: logical sequencing, cohesion well managed. Inserted "Moreover/Furthermore" between narrative beats would cap CC as mechanical.
  • LR: Narrative physical detail is vivid and exact: "my stomach dropped", "sit there watching the minutes pass", "arriving sweaty and breathless", "sitting helplessly in that bus". Each phrase fits the panic precisely rather than decorating it. Band 8 logic: flexible range, skilful uncommon use. A memorised "bitter experience" cliché in place of these fitted details would cap LR.
  • GRA: Past perfect setup ("I had planned everything"), past continuous and participles for unfolding action ("checking the time", "watching the minutes pass", "arriving sweaty and breathless"), and present habit in reflection ("I still leave extra time"). Wide range, majority error-free. Band 8 logic: varied structures with good control. Memorised story frames often collapse tenses mid-narrative; control holds here.

4. Grammar frames that carry ideas

  • Frame: Past perfect plan + "but" reversal ("I had planned everything carefully… but halfway there the bus simply stopped") — example: "I had planned everything carefully, leaving the house ninety minutes early". Job: sets up expectation so the disruption hits harder; reuse to open any late/stuck story.
  • Frame: "Looking back, X taught me to…" — example: "Looking back, that morning taught me to always have a backup route". Job: signals the reflection bullet and converts story into lesson; reuse for any "what you learned" ending.

5. Outline to copy (not sentences)

  1. Orient: name the event + occasion + lateness in one line; add the careful plan.
  2. Beat 1: rising tension — waiting, checking, the bad news.
  3. Beat 2: what you did — the escape or arrival.
  4. Reflect: outcome + lesson + present habit it created.

6. 10-minute task + do not copy

Task: Retell this monologue from the outline only using a real late-or-stuck experience of yours. Keep the past-perfect-plan opening and the "Looking back" lesson frame, with your own details and timings.
Do not copy: "I eventually jumped out and ran the last kilometre in my formal shoes" — works only here because the interview clothes make the run costly; reuse the move (costly last effort), not the sentence.
